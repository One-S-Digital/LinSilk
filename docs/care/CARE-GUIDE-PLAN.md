# LINSILK Care Guide: research and plan

Status: plan for review, 2026-11-16. Nothing is built yet. House decisions are in §0 and override anything below that differs.

## 0. House care rules (decided by the owner, 2026-11-16)

| Cloth | First choice | Also allowed | Never |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Silk, plain (LS-S)** | Hand wash, cool, pH-neutral silk detergent | Machine: delicate cycle, 30 °C, mesh bag, lowest spin (not the first option) | Bleach, softener, wringing, tumble dryer |
| **Silk, jacquard and brocade (LS-J, LS-B)** | Professional cleaning only | – | Home washing |
| **Linen (LS-L)** | **First wash: professional clean** (the cloth is not pre-washed). After that: gentle machine cycle, 30 °C | Hand wash, cool | Hot wash, bleach, tumble dryer |
| **Cashmere pants (LS-C)** | Hand wash, cool | Machine: wool cycle, 30 °C | Hot water, tumble dryer, wringing |
| **Silk ties** | Never washed: spot blot, then a professional cleaner | Steam from a distance | Washing, hot iron flat on the tie |

- **Labels:** each garment has a sewn-in fibre label; there is **no care label with symbols**, so this page is the only care information. The symbol strip on the page shows the house rules above.
- **Ties:** included as the fourth tab.
- **QR:** one code on every box, to `bylinsilk.com/care` (the address must never change once printed).
- **Open point (advice, not decided):** machine washing woven, tailored cashmere pants is not covered by published care advice (it is written for knitwear). Recommended before launch: wash one pair on the wool cycle and measure it, and say "first choice: hand wash" on the page.
- **Why linen's first wash is professional:** unwashed linen typically shrinks 3–10 % on its first wash, mostly in length, even in cold water; for a made-to-measure fit that can be several centimetres. The page explains this in one plain line so customers follow it.

## 1. The job of the page

The box has no card, no booklet and no swing tag text, only one QR code. That makes this page the whole after-sales experience. Most visitors arrive:

- **on a phone**, seconds after opening the box, often with the piece in their hands;
- **with one cloth in mind** (the one they just bought), not all three;
- **later, with a problem**: a crease before dinner, a wine spot, a first wash, putting cashmere away for summer.

So the page has three jobs, in this order:
1. **Welcome**: a calm, beautiful first screen that feels like the unboxing continues.
2. **Get them to their cloth in one tap**, then give clear, illustrated care steps.
3. **Answer the "help, now" moments fast**: stains, creases, storage, symbols.

Success = a customer can, on a phone, find how to wash, dry, press and store their piece in under 10 seconds, without zooming, and the page looks like it belongs to the house.

## 2. Research: what the care should say

The guidance below is the consensus across brand and specialist sources (listed in §11). Where sources disagree, this plan picks the gentler option. **Items marked ⚑ need a house decision from you before copy is final (see §9).**

### Silk (dresses, gowns, skirts, blouses, sleepwear; plain, jacquard and brocade)
- **Wash:** by hand in cool water, at most 30 °C, with a pH-neutral detergent for silk or delicates. No enzymes, no bleach, no softener. Short soak (a few minutes), swish gently, never scrub. Rinse in cool water.
- **Dry:** never wring. Press water out, roll in a clean towel, then dry flat or on a padded hanger, out of sun and away from radiators. No tumble dryer.
- **Press:** inside out, while **dry**, low (silk) setting, with a pressing cloth. Do not spray water on silk while ironing: it can leave water marks. A steamer held a few centimetres away is the easiest daily option.
- **Wear:** let perfume and deodorant dry fully before dressing. Salts in deodorant and sweat weaken silk at the underarms and leave marks; air silk between wears rather than washing after every wear.
- **Stains:** do not dab a fresh spill with plain water (can set a ring). Blot, never rub (wet silk fibres break easily). For oil, wine, deodorant or anything set: a professional cleaner.
- **Store:** cool, dark, dry. Padded hangers for dresses and blouses; gowns hung inside a breathable cotton cover, never plastic. Out of sunlight, which fades and weakens silk.
- ⚑ **Jacquard and brocade silks** are commonly sold as dry-clean only (woven patterns and metallic yarns can pucker or shift). Recommend: "professional clean" for LS-J and LS-B, hand wash for LS-S plain silk.

### Linen (all menswear, women's waistcoat and pants sets)
- **Wash:** cool, 30 °C, gentle cycle or by hand, mild detergent, inside out, ideally in a mesh bag. No bleach (yellows and weakens the fibre). Heat is the main cause of shrinkage.
- **Dry:** air dry, shaken out and reshaped, out of strong sun. No tumble dryer: it causes shrinkage and set-in creases.
- **Press:** while still slightly damp, inside out, medium to hot (two dots). Dark linen pressed on the reverse to avoid shine.
- **Wear:** creases are the nature of linen. Say so with pride (the brand already says it on /cloth). Linen softens with every wash.
- **Store:** hang shirts and jackets on wide hangers, fold pants loosely; breathable, never sealed plastic for long periods.
- ⚑ **Is LINSILK's linen pre-washed?** For made-to-measure this matters most: unwashed linen can shrink a few percent at first wash, which would spoil a fit taken to the centimetre. If not pre-washed, the guide must say "hand wash cool, or professional clean, for the first wash."
- ⚑ The site currently tells customers to **hand wash** linen. Most linen houses allow a 30 °C gentle machine cycle. Decide which the house says.

### Cashmere (woven tailoring: pants 071–074)
- Important: almost all published cashmere care is written for **knitwear**. LINSILK's cashmere is **woven, tailored pants** (pressed crease, waistband, pockets). Tailored woven cashmere is normally **professionally cleaned**, not hand washed, so it keeps its crease and shape.
- **Between wears:** brush with a soft clothes brush in the direction of the nap, hang by the hem from clip hangers so creases fall out, and rest a day between wears.
- **Refresh:** steam from a distance on the reverse; never place a hot iron directly on the face (shine and flattened nap). If pressing, use a damp pressing cloth on a wool setting.
- **Pilling:** natural at friction points; remove with a cashmere comb, lightly and rarely.
- **Store:** clean before storing (moths are drawn to body oils and food traces), in a breathable cotton bag with cedar or lavender, in a cool dark place. Air and check in spring.
- **Spills:** blot, never rub; professional clean.
- ⚑ Confirm the house line: "professional clean only" for cashmere pants (recommended), or allow careful hand wash.

### Silk ties (075, 076)
- **Never wash.** Untie by reversing the knot, never by pulling the narrow end through.
- Rest flat or rolled loosely overnight; store rolled in a drawer or draped on a rack, out of sun.
- Creases: steam from a distance or hang in a steamy bathroom. Never press flat with a hot iron (flattens the rolled edge).
- Spills: blot, then a professional cleaner who handles ties.

### Care symbols (ISO 3758 / GINETEX)
- Five families in this order: **wash (tub), bleach (triangle), dry (square), iron (iron, dots = heat), professional care (circle)**.
- Only about 1 in 5 Europeans read the professional-care circle correctly (GINETEX survey). A tap-to-explain symbol decoder is genuinely useful.
- The symbols are a GINETEX trademark; we draw our own versions in the house line style and show them as explanations, not as a label.

### QR landing-page practice
- One purpose per code; the URL must be final before the boxes are printed.
- Mobile first, fast, the most important thing on the first screen, no autoplay video or heavy scripts.
- Match what the packaging promises ("Care for your piece" → the page opens on care, not a shop).

### Current site to align
Today the piece pages, `/care/questions` and `lib/garments.ts` all say **"cool hand-wash, dry flat out of direct sun"** for all three cloths. Once the house decides §9, those lines are updated to match, so the site never says two different things.

- The existing `/care/shipping`, `/returns`, `/measuring`, `/questions` stay where they are and are linked from the bottom of the guide.

## 5. Section by section

**1. Welcome hero.** Full-bleed on `--ink` with a slow `HeroMedia` crop of `cloth-folded.webp` (or a new still life of a folded piece on tissue). Kicker "Care", title in Newsreader, e.g. "Made for you. Kept for years." (final wording written fresh under the copy rules, §8). One line under it: "Choose your cloth below." Short: under 70% of the phone screen so the cloth cards peek in.

**2. Choose your cloth.** Four large tappable cards in a 2×2 grid on phone, a row of four on desktop. Each card is the real cloth texture (clean swatch renders already in `public/swatches/`, e.g. a neutral LS-S, LS-L, LS-C) under a soft scrim, with the cloth name and a one-line character ("cool, fluid, light" / "airy, crisp, softening" / "soft, warm, tailored" / "rolled edge, hand-tied"). Selecting a card updates the URL hash and glides to section 3. Neutral swatches only, no colour named.

**Sticky cloth switcher.** Once past the cards, a slim segmented control (Silk | Linen | Cashmere | Ties) sticks under the header, with a gold underline that slides between choices. Always one tap to change cloth.

**3. At a glance.** A row of six large custom care symbols, each with a two-word caption ("30° by hand", "No bleach", "Dry flat", "Iron low", "Professional clean", "No dryer"). Under it, three one-line golden rules for that cloth. This is the screen people will screenshot.

**4. Wash.** Numbered steps (01, 02, 03…) as cards in the existing `DragRail` (swipe on phone, grid on desktop). Each card: a line illustration that draws itself in (reusing `RuleDraw`), a short title, two lines of text. Silk: basin & cool water → a capful of silk wash → swish, don't scrub → rinse cool → press, never wring → roll in a towel. Cashmere and ties show a single card instead: "Professional care", with what to tell the cleaner.

**5. Dry.** Small illustrated panel: flat on a towel / padded hanger / out of sun, plus a "never" chip for the tumble dryer.

**6. Press & steam.** A heat dial graphic: an iron outline with 1, 2, 3 dots; the chosen cloth's setting lights in gold (silk 1, linen 2, cashmere 1-2 with cloth). Beside it a steamer diagram with a distance marker ("a hand's width away"). Short tips: inside out, pressing cloth, damp vs dry.

**7. Wear & refresh.** Small icon list: rest between wears, perfume before dressing, brush cashmere, comb pills, linen creases are part of its beauty, air rather than wash.

**8. Store.** Diagram pair: hanger (padded / clip-hem / wide) vs folded, with the chosen cloth's way highlighted. Below, a "Seasons" strip: before storing (clean), how (breathable cotton bag, cedar), where (cool, dark, dry), when to check (spring).

**9. Keep / Avoid.** Two columns with check and cross icons. Fast to scan, very visual.

**10. Stain first aid.** An interactive tool: choose a stain from icon chips (wine, coffee, oil & food, make-up, deodorant & sweat, water mark), see three short steps for the chosen cloth and a clear "When to stop and see a cleaner" note. Every answer starts with "blot, don't rub". No harsh chemicals suggested.

**11. The three cloths compared.** A tidy table (cards on phone): water, detergent, drying, iron dots, steam, storage, professional cleaning, the chosen cloth's column highlighted.

**12. Read the symbols.** A grid of about 20 symbols in five families (wash, bleach, dry, iron, professional). Tap one to see what it means in a bottom sheet on phone, a popover on desktop. A short note: "Your cleaner reads these; here is what they mean."

**13. Travel.** Three small cards: roll linen, layer silk in tissue, hang cashmere on arrival in a steamy bathroom.

**14. Help.** A dark closing band: "Something we haven't covered? Write to us" (email + WhatsApp, same contacts as the site), links to Shipping, Returns, Measuring, Questions. A quiet "Save this guide" (print stylesheet, one clean A4 page per cloth).

## 6. Visual design and UI

- **Same house look:** bone ground, ink text, gold hairlines, Newsreader serif headings, Archivo light body, Caveat used once or twice as a "tailor's note" (the site's chalk-mark device), the existing easing and `Reveal` / `RuleDraw` / `Stagger` motion. Nothing new in the palette except a soft `--sand` panel for sections that alternate.
- **Icon set:** the site has no icon library, so a custom set is drawn as inline SVG in one consistent style (24 px grid, 1.25 px stroke, round caps, `currentColor`, gold on hover/active). One `CareIcon` component, about 40 icons:
  - care symbols: wash by hand, wash 30, wash gentle, do not wash, bleach / no bleach, dry flat, line dry, drip dry, dry in shade, no tumble, iron 1/2/3 dots, no iron, steam, professional dry clean P/F, wet clean W, do not dry clean;
  - actions: basin, detergent bottle, swirl, press (palms), towel roll, padded hanger, clip hanger, wide hanger, fold, cotton bag, cedar block, clothes brush, cashmere comb, steamer, pressing cloth, sun (avoid), radiator (avoid), perfume, travel case, tissue;
  - stains: wine glass, coffee cup, oil drop, lipstick, droplet (water), underarm;
  - UI: check, cross, chevron, arrow, info, print, mail, chat.
- **Illustrations:** a dozen slightly larger line drawings (the wash steps, hanger vs fold, heat dial, steamer distance) in the same stroke, gold and ink only, with a draw-on animation that respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Texture:** real cloth swatch renders on the cloth cards and as faint section headers, so each cloth feels like itself.
- **UI elements:** cloth cards, sticky segmented switcher, numbered step cards, symbol chips, heat dial, keep/avoid checklist, stain chips + result panel, comparison table, symbol decoder bottom sheet, accordion for "more detail", dark help band, print button.
- **Phone first:** large tap targets (at least 44 px), text at 17 px, nothing that needs zoom, one-hand reach for the switcher, sections short enough to scan.

## 7. Build approach

- `app/care/page.tsx` (+ the `[locale]` route entries) renders `CareGuideView` with `lang`.
- Copy in `lib/care-guide-i18n.ts` (EN source, DE-CH and FR like `home-i18n.ts`), one typed object per cloth so all four tabs share one layout.
- Components in `components/care/`: `CareIcon`, `ClothCards`, `ClothSwitcher`, `AtAGlance`, `StepRail`, `HeatDial`, `StoreDiagram`, `KeepAvoid`, `StainFirstAid`, `ClothTable`, `SymbolDecoder`. The page is a server component; only the switcher, stain tool and decoder are small client components.
- **SEO/GEO:** title "Silk, linen and cashmere care | LINSILK", `HowTo` JSON-LD per cloth (wash, dry, press), linked from the footer and from every piece page's care line.
- **Speed:** no new libraries, SVG inline, swatch images small WebP, no tracking beyond the existing `TrackEvent` (log cloth chosen and stain looked up, useful to know what customers ask).
- **Accessibility:** real buttons and tabs with ARIA, every icon has a text label, contrast checked on the dark band, reduced-motion respected.
- **Update existing lines:** piece-page care line, `/care/questions` answer and `lib/garments.ts` FAQ changed to match the final house rules and link to `/care`.
- **Verify:** run it locally, check at 375 px, 768 px and desktop, every cloth tab, the stain tool, the symbol decoder, German and French, the print view, and a Lighthouse mobile run. Screenshots for your review before anything is pushed.

## 8. Copy rules that apply

British spelling; "pants" not "trousers"; no colour names; never "satin"; never "hand-finished" or "handcrafted"; viewer-facing words (soft, light, ease, evening); no repeated slogan patterns; LINSILK named in key sentences; plain specifics over superlatives. The care steps themselves stay plain and practical, the welcome and section intros carry the warmth.
