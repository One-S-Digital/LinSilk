The Atelier

Nine people, one bench, no machine that finishes a seam.

The house works out of a single room in Hội An. Cloth is cut flat by hand against a paper pattern drawn for one client. Seams are closed on a machine; hems, facings, buttonholes and linings are finished by hand. A silk dress takes between eleven and sixteen hours at the bench, and it is checked twice against your measurement sheet before it leaves.

The Cloth

Silk and linen. Nothing else enters the room.

Mulberry silk from Bảo Lộc and wet-spun linen from Kortrijk. No polyester in the shell, the lining, the pocket bag or the thread. Synthetic fibre is cheaper to sew and it holds a press longer, which is precisely why most houses use it where you cannot see.

Natural fibre moves, creases and ages. A linen jacket worn through one summer will not look the way it looked in the photograph, and that is the point of it. Every bolt is logged with its mill and its date, and that record travels with the garment.

Upload your own design

If you already have the garment drawn, photographed or saved somewhere, send it here. The tailor works from what you give, so more detail shortens the conversation.

What to send
  • Front and back of the outfit, both views, as separate images.
  • Close-ups of anything that matters: collar, cuff, pocket, hem, closure.
  • Sketches, screenshots, pinned references or a photo of a garment you own.
  • Shoot flat or on a hanger in daylight — no flash, nothing folded out of view.
  • Mark lengths on the image if you know them, in centimetres.
  • JPG, PNG, PDF or HEIC, up to 20 MB each. Ten files at most.
